# Tank texturing scout: base materials, paint, camouflage, markings, wear

Scout lane, 2026-10-30. Read-only: no repo edits, builds or runs (glb/KTX2 headers parsed with a
throwaway Python script). Hull marks appear only as the view layer of the game's damage model.
Labels: **MEASURED** (read or computed from the shipped bytes or a cited
source line), **DERIVED** (arithmetic on labelled inputs), **ASSUMED** (a choice made here),
**CLAIMED** (stated by an outside source, not checked here).

## Gist

1. **Today:** the armour renders as flat, untextured substance colours (RHA blue-grey, Cast brown-grey…). Only the decor atlas (`Mat_Visual`, 3 × 4K, ≈ 64 MiB VRAM DERIVED, 80 % of the tank's texture memory), the rubber rims and the track link carry textures. The plates' UVs are collapsed leftovers, and the decor's UVs overlap everywhere (MEASURED), so neither can hold a unique bake without a fresh unwrap.
2. **Already decided in prose:** "paint is a runtime livery composed onto `paintable` substances". The `paintable` flag is parsed and read by nothing; the livery pass was never built.
3. **Trap:** the ballistic substance is the wrong key for the base metal look. It tracks alloy, never forming process, so cast sprockets are `MildSteel` like the stamped wheels.
4. **Viable architectures:** (A) a unique texture set baked per tank (Substance-style), with paint baked in; (B) **the tank-game consensus** (WoT, War Thunder, Armored Warfare, Dagor): a baked base per part, plus runtime paint (flat colour and finish) and runtime camouflage (one shared tiling pattern and a palette), both limited by a coverage mask, with non-metal parts kept out by that mask; (C) everything runtime-layered from a shared library of tiling surfaces, picked per pixel by per-tank ID, AO and curvature masks.
5. **Recommended direction: B, entered in stages.** Slice 1 is one generic tank `ExtendedMaterial` that puts livery data (paint, finish, camo palette, a shared pattern) on `paintable` surfaces, over the bases as they are now, with decor excluded. Later stages: a base layer on a new unique UV set (AO and curvature bakes, tiling finishes), then a decor rework. The same material is the "step 2" shader the hull marks need (#144), and it serves #158's merges.
6. **Owner questions:** (Q1) camouflage as one fixed historical scheme per tank, or player/nation/season customization? (Q2) base layer hand-authored per tank, or composed by the build from a shared library? (Q3) what picks a surface's base look: the substance, or an authored finish? (Q4) non-metal decor: one excluded atlas, or shared look materials, and does any of it become ballistic? (Q5) wear and grime static, or dynamic?

## 1. Current state (read 2026-10-30, branch lane/agent-a0561122fdcbf62fc at f832a1e)

### 1.1 What the view glb carries (MEASURED by parsing `assets/tiger_1/tiger_1.glb`'s JSON chunk)

- 13 glTF materials, 9 images, 233 primitives in 230 mesh records; 62 primitives hang on scene nodes,
  171 are unreferenced LOD rung records (ADR-0035: rungs are extra mesh records in the same file).
- 172 primitives carry no glTF material = the 171 rungs + `Link_Box` (hidden by the game). The rungs
  are not unpainted: at bind, every rung **clones its rung-0 primitive's material**
  (`src/geometry_lod.rs:18-21`, the clone at `:429-441`). So in game, every surface renders with one
  of the 13 materials.
- Ten materials are **flat PBR factors, no textures**: the substance materials `RHA` (base colour
  0.35/0.40/0.45), `Cast`, `GunSteel`, `MildSteel`, `EngineBlock`, `Ammunition`, `Flesh`, plus
  `Mat_RunningGear_Paint`, `Mat_Collider`, `Mat_Shadow_Proxy`. These come from the linked substance
  library `assets/materials/materials.blend`, whose `cc.txt` says the substances are "original
  flat-PBR datablocks authored in-project". **The armour the player sees is the substance's flat
  legibility colour: that is why the tank reads unpainted.**
- Three materials carry textures (all UASTC KTX2 + Zstd, full mips; dimensions MEASURED from the
  KTX2 headers, on-disk sizes MEASURED):

  | material | worn by | maps | size | on disk |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `Mat_Visual` | `Hull_Decor` (35.0 m², 32,061 verts), `Turret_Decor` (6.0 m²) | albedo, metal-rough, normal | 3 × 4096² | 49.3 MB |
  | `Rubber` (a substance, from `materials.blend`) | road-wheel rims | albedo, roughness, normal | 3 × 2048² | 9.6 MB |
  | `Mat_Track_Link` | track shoe | albedo, roughness, normal (ambientCG Metal046B, CC0) | 3 × 512² | 0.8 MB |

- **VRAM today, DERIVED:** UASTC transcodes to BC7 (desktop) or ASTC 4×4 (Apple) at 1 byte/texel
  (repo CLAIM, `scripts/encode-tank-ktx2.sh:35-37`), +1/3 for mips. `Mat_Visual` 3 × 21.3 MiB = 64 MiB;
  `Rubber` 16 MiB; link 1 MiB; **≈ 81 MiB per tank type**, shared by every instance of that type.
  80 % of it is the decor atlas.
- One sampler for every texture: linear/trilinear, default REPEAT wrap (MEASURED, `samplers[0]`).

### 1.2 The UVs cannot carry a unique bake today (MEASURED)

- Armour plates keep TEXCOORD_0 from the original model's atlas, at roughly 260–330 px/m against a
  4K texture (DERIVED from MEASURED UV/surface area), **and several are collapsed**:
  `Hull_Side_Track_L/R` zero UV area, `Hull_Side_Lower_L/R` 21 px/m on 11.3 m², `Turret_Top` 26 px/m,
  `Turret_Bottom` 15 px/m. Any texture on the plates needs a fresh unwrap.
- `Mat_Visual`'s UVs **fully overlap**: a 256² rasterization of all 15,955 triangles (V wrapped, as
  the REPEAT sampler does) covers 100 % of texels, every covered texel by more than one triangle,
  mean 2.68 layers. V runs to 1.97. So the decor atlas is stacked/tiled, not a unique unwrap: no
  per-texel AO, wear or grime bake can live in UV0. That is probably part of why it reads messy
  (INFERRED).
- The road wheels already carry TEXCOORD_1 (4 primitives, MEASURED), and Bevy 0.19 lets any
  `StandardMaterial` texture choose `UvChannel::Uv0`/`Uv1` (`bevy_mesh-0.19.1/src/mesh.rs:2533`).
  A second, unique UV set for bakes is supported by the engine.
- The repo's earlier "house standard" of 322 px/m for the hull/turret atlas
  (`.agents/blender/track-texturing/HANDOFF-track-texturing.md:105-111`, 2026-07-27) matches the
  plates' measured density; the link was tiled at 1,463 px/m.

### 1.3 How substance relates to the look today

- **Material = membership** is stated in the design doc, not an ADR:
  `.agents/docs/design/armor-penetration-and-damage.md:1179-1197` (§12 superseding note: "MEMBERSHIP
  = material, per primitive … decor must never wear a substance material … IDENTITY = free-form
  names, never parsed"). `assets/materials/materials.ron:93-99` repeats it ("THIS FILE IS THE
  CLASSIFIER"), ADR-0035:17-18 cites it for the sim artifact. **Doc defect:**
  `scripts/tank/trio.py:289` attributes it to "ADR 0007", which is the superseded skein ADR and does
  not say it.
- **Paint is already decided to be a runtime livery:** "Paint is a **runtime livery** composed onto
  `paintable` substances — never a material assignment in the source"
  (`armor-penetration-and-damage.md:1196-1197`); "substance is authored per-plate, paint is a runtime
  livery" (`materials.ron:5-6`). Each `Hard` substance carries `paintable`
  (`materials.ron:90-91`: exterior steels yes, interior no; RHA/GunSteel/Cast/MildSteel true;
  EngineBlock/Ammunition/Rubber/TungstenCarbide/Wood false). Parsed into
  `HardSubstance::paintable` (`src/substances.rs:180-182`), carried as `BallisticVolume::substance`
  "for … the paintable-livery pass" (`src/ballistics.rs:1313-1320`). **Nothing reads it** (MEASURED:
  no `.paintable` read outside the parser and fixtures). The livery pass was planned and never built.
- **"Substance boundaries are geometry, never paint"** (`armor-penetration-and-damage.md:1546-1552`):
  a mixed part is one closed shell per substance, so substance is per-primitive, never per-face.
- **The substance is the wrong key for the base look.** A substance tracks alloy and heat
  treatment, "never the FORMING PROCESS" (`materials.ron:57-62`), and `MildSteel` deliberately
  covers stamped wheel bodies **and** cast sprockets/idlers (`materials.ron:77-84`). The visible
  base surface tracks exactly what the registry refuses to encode: cast vs rolled vs forged vs
  stamped, plus welds. Keying the base look on the substance would render a cast sprocket as plate.
- **The library already pairs one substance with a look:** `Rubber` is a substance datablock in
  `materials.blend` that carries a packed 2K texture set (`assets/materials/cc.txt`). Precedent for
  "a substance has a base look", and for a shared, linked look library.
- Decor wears non-substance materials (`Mat_Visual`, `Mat_RunningGear_Paint`), so the decor's real
  materials (rope, logs, canvas, grilles, exhaust, tools) exist only as pixels in one atlas. No data
  says which is which.

### 1.4 Pipeline constraints

- One door: `scripts/tank/build.py` → view glb + sim glb + certificate (ADR-0035:9-27). The sim glb
  strips textures and UVs (`src/tank/spawn.rs:108-115`, `scripts/tank/trio.py:284-290`): the look is
  view-only by construction, and the server never loads it.
- The KTX2 derivation reads each image's colour role off the glTF slot that samples it and refuses
  any image without exactly one known role (`scripts/tank/glb_ktx2.py:61-66`, `D.KTX2_MIPS`). A
  custom mask texture outside glTF's standard slots cannot ride the glb as is; it would ship as a
  loose KTX2 (the terrain's way, `src/terrain_blend.rs:154-162`) or the law would need a new role.
- `bevy_gltf` 0.19 honours `KHR_texture_transform` only on `base_color_texture`, so glTF-authored
  tiling must be baked into the UVs (HANDOFF-track-texturing.md:80-83, citing
  `bevy_gltf-0.19.0/src/lib.rs:118`; CLAIMED, not re-read). A shader-side tiling scale avoids this.
- glTF `extras` are not exported with the plain-default export (HANDOFF:85-88, CLAIMED). Per-primitive
  look data therefore rides the material name, a node-keyed RON facet, a vertex attribute or a
  texture; not Blender custom properties.
- ADR-0034:15-22: per-tank truth lives only in `<tank>.blend`, `<tank>.tank.ron`, `materials.ron`
  and the LOD data. A livery or look table is fine as data that describes; a shader branch per tank
  is not.
- ADR-0014 / AGENTS.md: sim state is built at spawn from data, never from an asset. A livery is view
  state, but a **player-chosen** livery is per-player data other clients must learn, i.e. it rides the
  wire, not the glb.
- The owner's earlier preference (2026-07-27): "Yan explicitly wants texturing handled at
  Blender/export level, not in game code" (HANDOFF:124-125). Stated for mip handling then; whether
  it still holds for a livery shader is a question, not a fact.

## 2. Survey: how others do it (all sources read 2026-10-30; numbered sources in §6)

### 2.1 Tank games

**World of Tanks (Wargaming).**
- **Regions.** Four parts (chassis, hull, turret, gun), each with up to 4 sub-regions, make a 16-bit
  ApplyArea mask. Each customization type is limited to a subset of regions:
  - paint goes on most regions;
  - camouflage goes only on the primary region of hull, turret and gun, never the chassis;
  - emblems, inscriptions and numbers go on specific sub-regions.

  CLAIMED [W0]. The official guide agrees: paint has 5 areas, camouflage only hull, turret and gun,
  per season [W1][W2]. The vehicle data gives each area its own `<tiling>` and `<exclusionMask>` [W3].
- **Base.** A baked PBR set per part: albedo `_AM`, normal `_ANM`, gloss/metallic `_GMM`, and `_AO`.
  It is not a trim sheet and not one tank-wide atlas. Wear, dust and edges are baked in; there is no
  runtime wear layer. CLAIMED/INFERRED [W0], suffixes [W5][W6]. An artist breakdown builds the base as
  raw metal → patina → primer → paint, with dust from AO and curvature, all in Substance [W10].
- **Paint** is a flat override per region (colour, gloss, metallic), not a texture. A nation's base
  look is a default paint. CLAIMED [W0].
- **Camouflage** is one shared tiling pattern recoloured by a palette of up to 5 colours. Each part
  sets its own tiling, scale and rotation. It samples the tank's UV0 (or a camo UV set), is limited
  by a region mask, and **also modulates gloss, metallic and normal**: it reads as a surface finish,
  not a sticker. Variants are seasonal. CLAIMED [W0]. A 2018 community reverse-engineering of the
  shader agrees [W5] (UNOFFICIAL):
  - RGBA mask × per-tank tiling × per-camo tiling, one palette colour per channel over the base paint;
  - per-pixel coverage stored in the AO map's alpha;
  - tracks found by AO = 0;
  - a tiled scratch detail map on metal;
  - grilles alpha-tested against the normal map's spare R channel.
- **Exclusions.** Non-metal parts (rope, logs, canvas, grilles, exhaust) sit outside the camouflage
  regions and keep their authored material. Styles can restyle them (`nonTankMaterials`). CLAIMED [W0].
  A decoded config excludes materials from camouflage by name pattern (`*inside`, `tank_bottom_mat`,
  `*equipment`) [W4].
- **Decals and styles.** Decals are UV-placed stickers on emblem sub-regions, plus projected decals
  (at most 9, at most 3 per area) placed at predefined slots [W0][W2][W8]. The customization layers
  run paint → camouflage → emblems/inscriptions → "effects" (ageing) → styles that bundle them [W1].
- **Cost.** Everything composites in **one material**. The camouflage normal applies only up to
  LOD 1. Camouflage, decals and insignia are shared library assets, paid for once. CLAIMED/INFERRED
  [W0]. Numbers, CLAIMED: the HD IS-3 is about 50k polygons [W9]; one 3D style is 68,595 triangles
  with two 4K and three 2K UV sets [W10].

**War Thunder (Gaijin).**
- **Base.** An albedo per part (`…_body/turret/gun`), and tracks have their own [T1]. The normal map
  packs R = inverted roughness, G = normal X, B = metalness, A = normal Y [T1].
- **Camouflage.**
  - A "scalable" camouflage is one seamless tiled texture swapped in. The player can rotate and
    scale it [T1][T2].
  - The **part albedo's alpha is the camouflage coverage**: black lets the camouflage show, white
    excludes it. Rollers and the turret roof are excluded this way [T1].
  - A "fixed" camouflage is a hand-painted albedo per part, the per-tank escape hatch [T1].
  - Recommended base textures are ≥ 2048 for ground vehicles, power-of-two up to 4096 (CLAIMED [T1]).
- **Customization extras.**
  - 2 decal slots, 4 with premium [T2][T5].
  - 3D "decorations" (jerrycans, foliage) as separate objects [T2].
  - A worn-to-pristine condition slider [T2].
  - Camouflage nets inherit the camouflage texture [T4].
- **Dynamic mud** takes the terrain material under the tank, washes off in water and shakes off on
  firing [T6].
- **Engine source.** Gaijin's published Dagor shader **forces the camouflage weight to 0 across a hull
  mark's rim** (`hull-mark-rendering.md:67-70`, CLAIMED). Marks and camouflage live in one shader.

**Dagor `dynamic_painted_by_mask` (Gaijin's engine docs)** [D1].
- Inputs: an albedo, packed normal/metal/smoothness, a **grayscale paint mask (black = no paint)**,
  an optional camouflage mask, and two colour parameters.
- The plainest form of the paint mask: rubber, rope and wood stay their base material because the
  mask is black there.

**Armored Warfare** [A1][A2].
- Camouflage tiles are RGBA: three colour channels plus a soft-edged overlay. A transparent channel
  shows the base paint.
- The same pattern looks different per tank because each tank's UVs differ.
- "Anything truly complex must be hand-painted" as a skin.
- Layers: base paint, decal, camouflage, skin.

**Arma 3** [R1][R2][R3].
- Camouflage is retextured by swapping whole textures on named selections (`hiddenSelections`).
- The super shader runs albedo, normal, detail and specular on UV0, and ambient shadow and macro on
  UV1. A second UV set for bakes is standard there.

**Company of Heroes 3** [C1].
- Grime used to be baked in. CoH3 "invested heavily in [a] complex vehicle shader" so dirt matches
  the terrain driven over (press quoting Relic).

**Nothing usable found** for Squad, Gunner HEAT PC!, Foxhole, Hell Let Loose, Battlefield, Call of
Duty, Crossout or WARNO. Enlisted's use of Dagor is unconfirmed.

### 2.2 General techniques

- **Material-ID mask + tiling library.**
  - *The Order: 1886* composited a stack of "pure" templates (steel, gold…) through monochrome masks
    **offline** into 4 runtime maps. Separately it layered up to 4 materials **at runtime** on large
    tiled surfaces, blended by vertex colour. Masks can be auto-generated, e.g. dirt from a cavity
    bake [G1].
  - Unity HDRP LayeredLit takes up to 4 layers through an RGBA mask, optionally modulated by vertex
    colour, height-blended and triplanar. A full stack is about 22 textures (DERIVED) [G2][G3].
  - Unreal Material Layers [G4]. Substrate warns that cost scales with the number of BSDFs and falls
    back to parameter blending [G5].
  - *CoD: IW*, *Gears 4* and *Uncharted 4* talks exist but went unread (UNVERIFIED) [G6–G8].
- **Trim sheets** suit repeated details: weld beads, rivet strips, grille slats, brackets. Insomniac's
  "Ultimate Trim" [G9]. Large cast or rolled plates suit a tiling material plus a unique mask better.
- **Detail maps.** HDRP packs albedo variation, a detail normal and smoothness into one extra
  texture [G12]. Normal blending costs 5–8 ALU instructions (UDN / Whiteout / RNM, CLAIMED [G13]).
  StandardMaterial has no detail slot, so a detail map needs an `ExtendedMaterial` [G17].
- **Baked vs runtime.**
  - Baked: the cheapest shader (stock StandardMaterial, about 4 samples), and any look. Costs: memory
    per tank type *and per paint variant*, a re-export for every repaint, and close-up sharpness
    capped by texel density.
  - Runtime: variants are parameters, and detail no longer depends on the unique map's resolution.
    Costs: more samples and ALU, plus shader work [G1][G14].
- **Camouflage projection.**
  - A mask channel on the unique UV is the cheapest option, fixed per tank type.
  - A pattern on UV0/UV1 needs a decent unwrap.
  - Triplanar costs 3 samples per projected texture and must be **object-space**: world-space
    "swims" on a moving tank [G11].
  - Palette: mask weights × colour uniforms, so a nation swap is a parameter change. Per-instance data
    via a material per tank (Bevy 0.19 bindless batches these on desktop [G24]) or a `MeshTag` u32
    [G27].
- **Decals.** Unreal DBuffer decals need a depth prepass; mesh decals are floating geometry [G15].
  Bevy 0.19 options:
  - `ForwardDecal` is an alpha-blended quad that needs `DepthPrepass` [G16]. Overmatch already uses
    it for hull marks.
  - `ClusteredDecal` needs the non-default `pbr_clustered_decals` feature, bindless, **not macOS/iOS**,
    one sampler, 3-channel normals [G16][G19]. Ruled out while macOS ships.
  - Markings can instead be UV-placed inside the tank shader, the way WoT does it.
- **Wear, edges, grime.**
  - Substance generators run off baked curvature and AO (16-bit bakes recommended) [G14].
  - At runtime, curvature and AO sit in mask channels thresholded by a uniform, so "condition" becomes
    a parameter (DERIVED design).
  - Lower-hull mud: object-space height × AO × a shared mud set (DERIVED design; WT and CoH3 make it
    terrain-driven [T6][C1]).
- **Bevy 0.19 facts** that matter here (verified by the techniques lane against the v0.19.0 tag and
  docs.rs):
  - StandardMaterial has 6 texture/sampler pairs ungated, up to 15 with optional features [G18].
  - Per-texture `UvChannel::Uv0/Uv1`, but **one `uv_transform` applies to both UV0 and UV1**
    (`pbr_fragment.wgsl`) [G18]. Tiling UVs must be authored at final scale, or tiled in a custom
    shader.
  - Extension bindings start at 100 [G27].
  - UASTC KTX2 needs the `basis-universal` feature (on, `Cargo.toml:17`) and falls back to RGBA8 (4×
    memory) where the GPU lacks BC7/ASTC [G20].
  - wgpu's portable floor is 16 sampled textures per stage; native uses the adapter's limits [G21][G22].

### 2.3 Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Base baked per part, paint and camouflage at runtime.** Every tank game with published
   material does this (WoT, WT, AW, Dagor). The hand-painted unique skin survives only as an escape
   hatch for complex historical schemes (WT fixed camouflage, AW skins, WoT 3D styles).
2. **Camouflage is a shared tiling RGBA pattern plus a palette of 2–5 colours.** Its per-tank cost is
   a tiling scalar and a coverage mask stored in a **spare channel of a texture the tank already
   has**: WoT the AO alpha, WT the albedo alpha, Dagor its paint mask.
3. **Non-metal parts are kept out by that mask or by their own material.** Tracks have their own
   material, rubber and tools are masked off, grilles are alpha-tested, nets inherit the
   camouflage, and loose gear is often a separate object. None of them gets a "paint" treatment of
   its own.
4. **Decals are few (2–9 per tank) and slot-placed.**
5. **Wear has moved from fully baked to a condition parameter, and dirt to terrain-driven.**
6. **One material per tank** (WoT): layering is per-pixel ALU, not extra passes or draws.

## 3. The options for Overmatch, priced

Assumptions (ASSUMED): "per part" means hull, turret and gun+chassis; 10 tank types among the 30 in
the battle scene; BC7/ASTC at 1 B/texel, mips ×4/3. VRAM numbers are DERIVED from those.

| | **A. Baked unique** | **B. Baked base + runtime livery** (consensus) | **C. Runtime layered** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per type, VRAM | 3 parts × 4K set ≈ 192 MiB, plus 21 MiB per baked paint variant | 3 parts × 2K set ≈ 48 MiB (camouflage carries the close-up detail) | 2K normal + 2K RGBA mask ≈ 11 MiB |
| Shared, once | — | camouflage patterns, e.g. 10 × 1K ≈ 13 MiB | tiling library, e.g. 6 × 1K sets ≈ 20 MiB |
| 10 types | ≈ 1.9 GiB + variants: about half of LOW's 4 GB before terrain, shadow maps and the rest | ≈ 0.5 GiB | ≈ 0.13 GiB |
| Samples/pixel | ~4 (stock StandardMaterial) | ~5–8 (base, plus 1 pattern on UV1 or 3 triplanar, plus optional detail) | ~14 with 4 layers |
| Authoring per tank | unique unwrap + full texturing (Substance), per paint | unique unwrap + base texturing; paint and camouflage are data | unwrap + an ID/finish paint + automated AO/curvature bakes |
| Repaint / nation swap | re-bake, more memory | a data edit, zero memory | a data edit, zero memory |
| Engine work | none | one `ExtendedMaterial` | one `ExtendedMaterial` with arrays (the terrain pattern) |

For scale, terrain already pays `6 + 3k` texture requests per pixel over most of the screen
(`render-audit.md:102`). A tank covers a far smaller share of the screen, but that is ASSUMED, not
measured.

**The Order: 1886's split is a fourth path, and it fits the one-command build:** compose the base
from a shared library **offline**, in the tank build, into B's per-part textures. That gives B's
runtime cost with C's authoring cost. It is also a texturing platform, which is exactly what the
scope guard warns about, so it is not slice 1.

## 4. Recommendation (provisional; the owner decides)

**Shape: B, entered in stages, with each stage visible in play.**

1. **Slice 1, runtime livery.**
   - One generic tank surface material: `ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, TankLivery>`, the
     terrain's pattern.
   - At view bind, the glb's substance materials stay as identity (the sim needs the names). The view
     swaps them for the livery material, the way rungs clone materials today
     (`geometry_lod.rs:429-441`).
   - Coverage comes from data that already exists: `paintable` per substance. Decor (`Mat_Visual`)
     is excluded wholesale: rope, tools and the rest keep their authored atlas, as in WoT.
   - Livery data: flat paint colour and finish (roughness/metal) + a shared RGBA camouflage pattern
     + palette + tiling scale.
   - No UV work is needed if the pattern is projected **object-space triplanar** (3 fetches of one
     small texture). The plates' UV0 is collapsed, so UV-mapped camouflage must wait for stage 2.
   - Where the livery data lives (a view-only file vs `materials.ron`, which the server parses) is a
     slice-1 call.
   - This gives the owner camouflage on the tank and closes #144's "judge marks on real camo".
2. **Stage 2, base layer.** A unique UV1 per tank. Bake AO and curvature (Blender, headless, in the
   build) into one RGBA mask. That mask carries per-pixel camouflage coverage, edge wear and grime,
   and moves camouflage onto UV1. The look key for plates is decided here (Q3).
3. **Stage 3, decor.** Re-author the 4K atlas, whose UVs overlap and which costs 80 % of today's
   texture memory: either split it into shared look materials (rope, wood, canvas, grille, rusted
   steel; trim-sheet territory) or re-unwrap and repaint it.
4. **Markings** stay `ForwardDecal` or become UV-placed in the livery shader. Clustered decals are
   out while macOS ships.

Why B: every tank game with published material converged on it. It keeps a repaint as a data edit
(ADR-0034's "tanks are data"). It costs per tank type, never per instance. It matches the prose
decision already in the repo. And it is the same material that hull-mark step 2 and #158's merges
want.

## 5. Draft issue

**Title:** Tanks look unpainted: decide how a tank's surface is built (metal, paint, camouflage, markings, wear)

**Labels:** `enhancement`, `ready-for-human` (owner decision), `wayfinder:grilling`

**Body:**

> The fixture tank renders unpainted. Its armour draws in the flat colours of its ballistic
> substances (RHA blue-grey, Cast brown), with no textures. Only the decor atlas, the rubber rims and
> the track link are textured, and the decor atlas is the original model's: its UVs overlap
> everywhere, and it costs about 64 MiB of the tank's ≈ 81 MiB texture memory (DERIVED). The design
> doc already says "paint is a runtime livery composed onto `paintable` substances"
> (`armor-penetration-and-damage.md:1196`), and `materials.ron` carries the flag, but nothing reads
> it. Report: `.agents/scratch/<path>/tank-texturing-scout.md`.
>
> **What others do.** World of Tanks, War Thunder, Armored Warfare and Gaijin's Dagor all bake a
> base per part (wear included), then paint (flat colour and finish) and camouflage (one shared tiling
> pattern plus a palette) at runtime, limited by a coverage mask. Non-metal parts (rope, logs, canvas,
> grilles, rubber) sit outside the mask and keep their own material. One material per tank.
>
> **Options.**
> - A. A unique texture set baked per tank (Substance), paint baked in. Cheapest shader; ~192 MiB per
>   type at 4K plus memory per paint variant; a repaint means a re-bake.
> - B. A baked per-part base plus runtime paint and camouflage (the consensus). ~48 MiB per type at
>   2K plus a shared pattern library; a repaint is a data edit; one `ExtendedMaterial`.
> - C. Fully runtime layers from a shared tiling library, by per-tank ID/AO/curvature masks. ~11 MiB
>   per type; ~14 texture samples per pixel.
>
> **Proposed first slice (B, stage 1).** A generic tank livery material on `paintable` surfaces:
> flat paint and finish plus object-space camouflage from a shared pattern and palette, over today's
> bases, with decor excluded. No asset rework. Later stages: a unique UV1 with AO and curvature bakes,
> then a decor re-author.
>
> **Questions for the owner.**
> 1. Camouflage: one fixed historical scheme per tank, or player/nation/season customization? The
>    second puts livery on the wire.
> 2. Base layer: hand-authored per tank in Substance/Blender, or composed by the build from a shared
>    library? Does the 2026-07-27 wish ("texturing at Blender/export level, not in game code") still
>    hold for paint?
> 3. What picks a surface's base look: its ballistic substance (zero authoring, but a cast sprocket
>    is `MildSteel` and would look like plate), or an authored finish (cast, rolled, forged, stamped)?
> 4. Non-metal decor: one atlas excluded from paint, or shared look materials (rope, wood, canvas,
>    grille, rusted steel)? Does any of it become ballistic (a log is `Wood`)?
> 5. Wear and grime: static (baked, or a condition slider), or dynamic (terrain mud, as War Thunder
>    and CoH3 do)?
>
> **Related:** #144 (hull marks judged on real camouflage; its "step 2" shader is this material),
> #158 (merging static parts per material).
>
> **Acceptance (slice 1):** the owner approves stills of the Tiger in two liveries; a second livery
> is a data edit, with no code and no asset change; the frame-cost delta is MEASURED at 30 tanks.
> Tanks are data: no tank name in the material or its tests (ADR-0034).
