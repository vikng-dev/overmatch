# Tiger I (Pz.Kpfw. VI Ausf. E) — what each part was made of and how (construction and manufacture only)

Research date 2026-10-30. Subject is construction, not the game's damage model.

## Read this first: what the evidence is

- Jentz & Doyle, the Tigerfibel (D 656/27) and the British Tiger 131 reports could **not** be read directly: no page text was reachable through the tools. Every claim below that traces to them is **second-hand** (a web page quoting or paraphrasing them) and says so.
- Confidence tags: **A** = authoritative page read (Tank Museum, or a page that cites a named primary source). **B** = reputable secondary (Wikipedia, tiger1.info, Tank Encyclopedia, Tank Archives). **C** = forum, modelling, blog, or my own background knowledge, unverified. **GAP** = nothing found.
- Several tool answers came from a small summariser model reading truncated pages; wording was not re-verified against the originals. Treat B as "reported", not "confirmed".
- Dates in Jentz's modification list are first sighting on a finished vehicle; roll-out across output lagged months [S9].

## Factory finish

- **Primer.** Tank Museum (Henschel assembly, Takt 9): the last stage applied **"red oxide primer" and a base coat**, Panzer Grey or later Dunkelgelb [S1, A]. The Tiger 131 hull rebuild also used red-oxide primer over rust-proof grey [S16, A, but that is restoration, not factory]. Whether Zimmerit went on before or after primer: a forum says over the primer, before the base coat (C) [S17].
- **Base colour until Feb 1943.** Dunkelgrau, RAL 7021, from the first vehicles (Jul 1942) [S6, B]. A forum phrase of the same date range: "Dunkelgrau until 18 Feb 1943" [S17, C]. Tank Enc. adds the first 120-150 vehicles were Dunkelgrau [S6].
- **18 Feb 1943.** Order for **Dunkelgelb** base coat; the "Muster" shade then had no RAL number. Field/maintenance units applied Olivgrun RAL 6003 and Rotbraun RAL 8017 stripes over it; paint came as a paste thinned with liquids [S6, S17, B/C]. I did not see the order text itself.
- **Aug 1943.** Dunkelgelb standardised as **RAL 7028**: forum-only claim, and the changeover date is disputed between forums (C) [S17].
- **Who applied the camouflage.** From Feb 1943, field and maintenance units, not the factory [S6, S17]. A source claims Henschel delivered King Tigers in overall Dunkelgelb, camouflage left to the units (that is Tiger II, not Tiger I) [S17, C].
- **Tropen.** Tank Enc.: RAL 8020 brown and 7027 grey for the Russian Front by end 1943, and North Africa before shipping [S6, B]. Tiger 131 evidence conflicts, see below.
- **Zimmerit.** Paste (barium sulphate 40 %, PVA 25 %, ochre 15 %, zinc sulphide 10 %, sawdust 10 %; Mowilith 20), put on at the factory, patterned and hardened with blowtorches, adding days per vehicle [S18, B, citing Spalding, Jentz & Doyle, 21 Army Group Technical Report]. Applied to mid and late Tiger I (not early). Dates conflict, see below. Factory application ended 9 Sep 1944, field 7 Oct 1944 [S18]. Tiger I production ended Aug 1944 [S1], so late Tigers all carry it.
- **Post-Sep 1944.** The red-oxide-primer-with-patches regime (31 Oct 1944) cites Jentz's Panther book and applies to later vehicles only [S17, C]; no Tiger I was built after it.

## Open conflicts

1. **Zimmerit start.** Jentz modification list (as quoted): Aug 1943 [S8]. Tank Encyclopedia: factory from Aug 1943 [S6]. Wikipedia Zimmerit: Dec 1943 to 9 Sep 1944 [S18]. Wikipedia Tiger I: "late 1943" [S3]. Forums: Sep 1943. Use Aug-Sep 1943 as first appearance, with a lag in roll-out, matching Jentz's first-sighting rule [S9].
2. **Steel-tyred wheels.** Jan 1944 [S6] versus early Feb 1944, hull 250822 [S12, forum citing Jentz p.144]. Tank Museum summary says Feb 1944 (search summary, not read in full) [S10].
3. **Turret roof 40 mm.** Mar 1944 [S3, S6] versus Sep 1943 [S7]. Tank Enc. text itself gives both: "judged insufficient by Sep 1943; uniform 40 mm from Mar 1944".
4. **Mantlet cast or rolled.** Tank Encyclopedia says cast [S6]; Tank Archives (Volgin) says the hulls and turrets were "almost entirely" rolled and does not mention the mantlet [S5]. No primary source seen. **Treat as unresolved; Jentz & Doyle or the Tigerfibel decides it.**
5. **Smoke dischargers.** Tank Enc.: dropped Jun 1943 after grenades set off by gunfire; Jentz list: "stop S-Minen" Oct 1943 [S6, S8]. Different devices (smoke candle versus S-mine launcher): likely not a true conflict.
6. **Tiger 131 colours.** Tank Museum found traces of RAL 7008 on inner road wheels and RAL 8000 on the turret [S16, A]; Tank Enc. says Tropen brown/grey were RAL 8020/7027 [S6]. Different numbers for apparently the same scheme; the museum's finding is physical, so prefer it for Tiger 131 only.
7. **Steel grade/hardness.** Soviet 1945 test: lower front 100 mm = 285-302 HBW, a second 102 mm sample = 255-269 [S5, A]; required range for 85-120 mm plate was 220-265 HBW. The measured values exceed the specification, so one plate class cannot cover all Tigers. A British Tiger 131 hardness quote (320-340 HBW generally, front 285-300) comes from a second-hand search summary [S5b, C].
8. **Armour thickness numbers.** Wikipedia infobox 25-120 mm versus body text 200 mm (mantlet). Not a material issue.
