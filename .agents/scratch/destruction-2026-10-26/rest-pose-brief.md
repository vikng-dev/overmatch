## Context

`src/trees.rs::resting_tilt` (~line 439) derives a fallen piece's pose: pivot at the cut's bottom on the root's axis, the piece tilted toward `dir` by the smallest angle in [0°, 100°] at which any sample (each segment boundary above the cut and the tip, each lowered by its local radius) reaches the terrain, found by 24 bisections; nothing by 100° lies level at 90°. `derive_state` places the lying segments from it; `src/trees/view.rs::present_fall` turns the cut's trunk node to it over `fall_duration_s`. Two things are wrong, and the owner has decided the fix (2026-10-27): the pose stays a pure, scripted function of the word and the terrain — provisional, the destination is a server-simulated rigid body, NOT this task.

1. **Contact is not monotone in the tilt**, and a bisection assumes it is. A sample can touch an intermediate ridge at one tilt and clear it at a larger one (its height keeps dropping while it moves past the ridge onto lower ground), so the bisection can converge on a later contact and lay the piece through the ridge. The flat, slope and edge fixtures cannot show it.
2. **The piece never leaves the stump.** For a cut above the base the pivot is the stump's top, so the piece ends balanced on the stump with its tip on the ground, or, when nothing is reached by 100°, hanging level in the air at the stump's height. The owner wants it on the ground: the butt slides off the stump and the piece lies along the terrain.

## The law (decided — implement as stated; where it resists, report, do not redesign)

**`Resting` becomes the whole pose:** `Resting { hinge: f32, tilt: f32, seat: Vec3, dx: f32, dz: f32 }` — `hinge` the tilt about the cut at which the piece first meets the terrain (the view's first stage; for a cut at the base, the pose itself); `tilt` the final tilt, about the seat; `seat` the resting centre of the butt (the cut boundary) as an offset from the tree's base in WORLD axes, `Vec3::ZERO` for a cut at the base. `resting_tilt` becomes `resting_pose(body, base, cut, dir, height_at) -> Resting`, pure in the same inputs, libm-only trig, one fixed order of evaluation, so every peer and CI derive the same bits (that sentence is the invariant; keep it in the doc).

**The primitive — the first contact, bracketed then bisected.** One function (yours to name; suggestion `first_contact`) over a pivot, the samples above it, `(dx, dz)` and a range `[0, max]`: evaluate touching at `k × TILT_SCAN_STEP` for `k = 0, 1, …` up to `max` in increasing order; `k = 0` touching returns 0; the first touching `k` brackets `[(k−1) × step, k × step]`; bisect inside that bracket for `TILT_STEPS` halvings, returning the touching end; nothing touching at any step returns `None`. Constants with their derivations in the doc comments:
- `TILT_SCAN_STEP`: the scan can step over a feature narrower than `step × distance`; the bisection inside the bracket can still land one bracket late on a feature narrower than the step. Set the step so the feature a piece could visibly rest on cannot be skipped at the tip of the longest piece the species table allows (40 m, `assets/trees/species.ron`, MEASURED from the table): 0.25° gives 0.17 m at 40 m (DERIVED: 40 × 0.25° in radians). Kalinovo's grid cell is 0.366 m (MEASURED: 4096 samples across 1500 m, `assets/maps/kalinovo/level.json`), so a one-cell feature at that tip is wider than a step. Write the derivation; pick a different step only with a different derivation.
- `TILT_STEPS`: the halvings that take a step-wide bracket below one f32 ulp at the angles in range (DERIVED: 0.25° = 4.4e-3 rad; 2^16 halvings reach 6.7e-8, under the 1.2e-7 ulp near 1 rad); 16 suffices — more halvings past the ulp are no-ops, say so or stop at no progress.
- Samples: as now — each segment boundary above the pivot's boundary and the tip, lowered by its local radius; the pivot's own boundary is never a sample. The sample's xz goes to `height_at` as now (the oracle clamps at the map edge).

**Phase 1 — the hinge.** Pivot `base + Y × prefix[cut]`, range `MAX_TILT` (100°, as now, its doc kept), `hinge = first_contact(…).unwrap_or(FRAC_PI_2)` (nothing reached by 100° lies level, as now). For `cut == 0`: `tilt = hinge`, `seat = Vec3::ZERO`, done — a tree felled at the base keeps its butt at the base.

**Phase 2 — the seat, for `cut ≥ 1`.** `r = body.radius_at(body.prefix[cut])` (the cut's radius: the stump's top and the butt are the same boundary). The butt seats beside the stump on the fall's side: `seat.x = dx × 2r`, `seat.z = dz × 2r` (DERIVED: the butt's edge against the stump's edge), `seat.y = height_at(base.x + seat.x, base.z + seat.z) + r − base.y` (the butt rests on the terrain). Then `tilt = first_contact(pivot = base + seat, the same samples, range LYING_MAX_TILT).unwrap_or(FRAC_PI_2)`, with `LYING_MAX_TILT = 135°` (DERIVED: a lying piece follows a slope of up to 45° downhill; past that the ground is an edge and the piece lies level over it — the scripted law does not slide, the rigid body will; say exactly that in the doc, without the issue number). Claim to verify (DERIVED): on flat ground a sample touches when `cos tilt = (r_sample − r) / h_sample`, and the smallest such tilt wins; for the fixture trunk (`fixture_kind`, 2 m segments, radii 0.3 → 0.2 → 0.1 → 0.05) at cut 1 the tip (h 4, r 0.05) gives 92.15° before the 2 m boundary (h 2, r 0.1) at 92.87°; at cut 2 the tip (h 2, r 0.05 against r 0.1) gives 91.43°.

**Composition — the ONE source, the same bits in sim and view.** Keep `fall_rotation(resting, root, angle)`. Add `seat_in_root(resting, root) -> Vec3 = root.inverse() * resting.seat` (for a zero seat this is exactly zero). The sim's lying segment `k` in the root's frame: `translation = seat_in_root + turn * (Y × (prefix[k] − prefix[cut]))`, `rotation = turn`, with `turn = fall_rotation(resting, root, resting.tilt)` — so `derive_state`'s pivot line changes from `Y × prefix[cut]` to the seat, and its doc says the lying piece turns about the seat (the butt's resting centre), which is the cut's bottom only for a cut at the base.

**The view — two stages.** `present_fall` keeps its shape (the state, `Fell`, `Resting`, the clock, `fresh`/`behind`) and gains the second stage; `t = since(fell.tick) × dt` seconds:
- Stage A, duration `fall_duration_s(length)` as now: the cut's trunk node turns about its own origin from upright to `hinge` with the `phase²` easing as now; its translation is its standing one.
- Stage B, `cut ≥ 1` only (for a cut at the base `hinge == tilt` and the seat is zero, so the fall ends with A): duration `slide_duration_s(drop)` = the free fall over `drop` metres, `sqrt(2 × drop / 9.81)` clamped to [0.2, 1.0] s (DERIVED, g = 9.81 m/s²), `drop = max(prefix[cut] − seat.y, 0)` (a seat above the stump's top on an uphill fall drops nothing and takes the floor). Over B's phase the node's origin moves from its standing position to the seat with `phase²` (a drop accelerates) and its turn goes from `hinge` to `tilt` linearly. At B's end: translation exactly the seat, rotation exactly `fall_rotation(resting, root, tilt)` — the sim's lying rotation bits.
- The node's translation is in its parent's frame, and the chain is in ASSET metres under the view root's `Transform::from_scale(scale)` (`attach`); the chain carries no rotation (A5). The rest translation is `standing + (seat_in_root − Y × prefix[cut]) / body.scale`, where `standing` is the node's bound translation. Capture each trunk node's translation in `TreeViewNodes` at bind (the glb's own, never re-derived from the RON) and restore it when the tree stands again; never read it back from the node during the fall.
- `behind` compares translation as well as rotation against the frame's derived pose. A view binding to a tree long down presents both stages complete; a late ledger entry starts the fall at its tick (ADR-0037), as now.
- `fall_duration_s` and the clock stay; the sim never waits on the view (keep that sentence).
