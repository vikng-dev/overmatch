# Slowdown hunt
Subject: the video game's environment destruction and damage model; CPU and game state only.

**Causes.** High confidence: continuing rubble contacts repeatedly assess the complete source construction; optimized `weigh` consumes MEASURED 59–69% of active CPU samples. High confidence locally, moderate for the owner's incident: island-wide contact wakes reset timers, while a few rotating members keep an entire island awake. High confidence locally: idle CPU includes frame-frequency maintenance and scheduler wakes; the runner sleeps and its compute pool uses every available core. The owner's exact additional CPU share and late settling transition remain unattributed.

Symbol-only copies have identical executable `__text` bytes; the first cold-DWARF quiet sample yielded only three snapshots and is excluded. MEASURED 87 analytical tests and 269,428 native-collapse calls had zero mismatches. Operation counts and cache population are not gameplay verdicts. **Not fixed, largest remaining opportunities first.**

- **Construction graph work (T27).** Full-source allocation and traversal dominate both optimized profiles. The saved original/candidate probe proves the empty-joint subset can disappear without changing verdicts; it does not price a complete owned-subgraph/cache implementation. That larger change touches `Structure`, `Live::group`, `SectionCache` and `run_laws`; its cost is explicit invalidation after cracks, removals, support changes, changed presses and spin. T27 already owns that rewrite; carry this gate's equivalence proof into it, without adding an alternate law. T30 must measure the remainder after T27.
- **Automatic settling (T4).** MEASURED native collapse at 64 Hz, `edit`, no forced sleep: default at 70–80 simulated s had median 146 awake / 44 asleep, tick 50.71 ms, physics 3.21 ms. Raising the library's angular threshold to 1 rad/s yielded 0/131 at 70–80 s; setting its `TimeToSleep` to .1 s yielded 0/139. The guard alone retained default settings and yielded 0/125 at 170–180 s; all these changes also alter subsequent emergent piece counts. Default versus guard elapsed observation cost was 223.8 wall s for 80 simulated s versus 21.7 for 180. These are different resulting piles, not equal-population prices.
- **The guard is not production-proven.** MEASURED production shelling's final 20 wall s, `dev`: default 27 awake / 26 asleep, tick 7.92 ms, physics .68 ms; guard 44.5/7.5, tick 11.75 ms, physics .87 ms. The independent 1,000-body guard-only pile still had median 942 awake / 58 asleep at 110–120 simulated s, physics 7.50 ms. Its final 931-body island had 17 members over the angular threshold and no mature timer. Do not ship the guard or shorter timer from these results; resolve generic contact/island lifecycle and real-terrain settlement, rather than stack a debris freeze rule. The guard's focused stationary-contact witness fails without it; all six impact/support-loss/boundary tests pass with it. Both patches are saved; none is shipped.
- **Why the long tail can drop abruptly.** MEASURED default native snapshot at 80 simulated s: a 53-body island had one member above the angular threshold and 47 mature timers; another 53-body island had none above but zero mature timers. Across that run, 1,043 contact-wake requests reset 12,010 body timers. Whole-island sleep explains a sudden group-wide drop when the last blocker clears, but there are no owner body/island snapshots proving why its 1,000–1,100 s transition happened.
- **Idle CPU.** MEASURED optimized quiet profile: scheduler/queue/signal leaves 38.9%, collider propagation 11.1%, transform work 6.6%, child poses 5.3%, moved-AABB scans 2.4% of active samples. `ScheduleRunnerPlugin::run_loop(FRAME)` sleeps the remainder of its 4 ms frame; `FRAME` intentionally polls networking between 64 Hz ticks. `server_host::task_pool_options` assigns compute = available cores and async/IO = one each; no spin or unused-core sizing defect was found. CPU time sums threads, tick p50 is median wall time, and frames also run outside fixed ticks: subtracting p50 × Hz cannot precisely partition the owner's 30%. Profiling this Mac identifies the work, not its exact one-core-host share. Reducing polling is a latency-contract decision, not a no-brainer.
