# 8.8 cm KwK 36 L/56: round catalogue (2026-10-30)

**Subject:** this note collects round data for the Overmatch damage model: the issued rounds of the Tiger I's main gun, their specifications, and their fuzes. Effects are stated as potency or as effects on armour, walls and materials.

Labels: **CITED** means a value as printed in the named source. **DERIVED** means a unit conversion or ratio I computed. **READ** means a value taken from the repo's `assets/rounds/*.round.ron`. Unless a row says otherwise, imperial-to-metric conversions are DERIVED (1 lb = 0.4536 kg, 1 oz = 28.35 g, 1 ft/s = 0.3048 m/s).

## Per-round notes and disagreements

### Pzgr. 39 (armour-piercing with a filler)

- **Mass.** NV5 gives 10.2 kg. TM53 p.448 gives 22 lb (9.98 kg). TTT30 gives 10.2 kg (22.44 lb) for the Flak 41 projectile, which it says is "similar" to the one in the older 8.8 cm gun.
- **The 1943 Flak-pattern figures differ.** Brit43's "A.P. 38 (A.P.C.B.C.)" column reads 20.75 lb (9.41 kg), 2,657 ft/s (810 m/s), and a filling of "3" in a column headed "(lbs)", which is implausible as printed. E9-369A gives the Flak "8.8 cm Pzgr. m. Bd.Z." as 20.71 lb at 2,657 ft/s, with TNT at about 1.8 % of projectile mass (≈ 0.37 lb ≈ 170 g, DERIVED). TME30 gives Flak 18/36/37 AP as 21 lb at 2,600 ft/s (792 m/s). These are the older pattern of Flak AP round, not the KwK 36 Pzgr. 39 figures in NV5.
- **Filler.** The sources disagree:
  - TM53 p.448 is the only entry I found that names the Kw.K. 36: ¼ lb (≈ 113 g) cyclonite/wax 90/10, with a tracer marked "AB".
  - The 60 g figure is CITED only for the Flak 41 round: TTT30 says 60 g cyclonite, and TM53 p.438 says 2.12 oz (60.1 g) for the Flak 41 Pzgr. Patr. 39, a "small cavity" APCBCHE.
  - TM53 p.446 gives the older 8.8 cm Pzgr. ⅓ lb (151 g) of TNT/wax plus PETN/wax.
- **Fuze.** Base fuze Bd.Z. 5103 or Bd.Z. 5103/1 (TM53 p.448). The Flak 41 and Pak/KwK 43 versions (Pzgr. 39/1, 39/43) carry the Bd.Z. 5127 (TM53 p.439 for the Flak 41 Pzgr. Patr. 41, p.443 for the Pzgr. 39/43, p.447 for the Pzgr. 39/1; TTT30; HEA12 p.19). HEA12 describes the 5127 as an inertia collar that sheds a shear wire on impact and unlocks a spring striker. The 5127 with its tracer weighs 11½ oz (326 g); no delay figure is given.
  - For the older Flak Bd.Z., E9-369A says it is "a nondelay type", but "it is believed that a short delay is obtained" through the primer plunger and the gas jet. It gives the complete fuze as 2.18 lb.
  - D435 (general notes): "Der Bd. Z. ist ein Fertigaufschlagzünder meistens mit Verzögerung" ("the Bd.Z. is a ready-armed impact fuze, mostly with delay").
  - I found **no primary figure** for the 5103's delay time or for the plate thickness it needs to function.
- **Behaviour on light targets versus armour.** Fibel p.71: Pzgr. 39 "knackt Panzer und Scharten bis 2000 m" (defeats tanks and embrasures out to 2,000 m). E9-369A (the Flak AP round): effective against smaller concrete emplacements; eight well-grouped rounds at 800 m perforate 2 m of reinforced concrete. Nothing I read records how the Pzgr. 39's base fuze behaves on brick, timber or soil.

### Pzgr. 40 (subcalibre)

- TM53 pp.439–440 lists it for the Flak 36 and 41: 16.0 lb, core 4.25 lb, core diameter 1.406 in (35.7 mm), tracer, no fuze.
- Brit43 gives 16 lb, core 4.25 lb, core length 5.5 in (140 mm), and core diameter **1.375 in (34.9 mm)**. The two sources disagree on the core diameter.
- Fibel p.71: use it only when the Pzgr. 39 does not get through, out to 1,500 m. Because it flies flatter, set the sight 100 m short from 600 to 1,000 m and 200 m short from 1,100 to 1,500 m.

### Sprgr. L/4.5 (high-explosive) and its fuze

- **Mass and filler.** TM53 p.444–445 lists it for the Kw.K. 36 and the Flak 18/36/37: 20.5 lb, 2 lb amatol 60/40 poured, booster Wlf Np 10, fuze A.Z. 23/28. The Flak round in TM53 p.438 is 20.35 lb with 1.9 lb of 40/60 amatol. E9-369A gives 20.34 lb with the A.Z. 23/28 and 2.19 lb of TNT or 40/60 amatol. A 9.0 kg mass is quoted from Jentz (1996) through Wikipedia; I did not verify it.
- **Fuze A.Z. 23/28**, in the nose. It is a combined graze and direct-action percussion fuze with a wooden hammer in its nose channel (HEA12 pp.4–7, A.Z. 23 family). It has two selectable settings:
  - **o.V.** (ohne Verzögerung, slot at "O"): instantaneous. This is the setting as shipped.
  - **m.V.** (mit Verzögerung): the slot is turned 90° to "M"/"V", and the burst comes after a delay pellet.
  - **Delay time:** 0.11 s per E9-369A, but **0.1 s ("v.(0,1)") stamped on the fuze** per HEA12 p.8. TM53 p.442 lists "A.Z. 23/28 (0.15)" on the KwK 43's Sprgr. 43 (read from the OCR; I did not check the page image), so stamped values vary.
  - **Setting tool:** the setting is changed with a screwdriver or the "Stellschlüssel für A.Z. 23" (E9-369A). The Brit43 stowage list includes a "fuze key for percussion fuze (type A.Z. 23)". Fibel p.54: "Verzögerung nur mit dem Schlüssel einstellen! Nach dem Entladen wieder zurück auf O.V. … sonst versagt sie!" ("Set the delay only with the key! After unloading, set it back to O.V., or it will fail!")
- **Sensitivity.** The A.Z. 23/28 is not bore-safe and is "particularly sensitive". Small branches or leaves in front of the muzzle can set it off early (E9-369A pars. 65c, 70a). Fibel p.55 gives the matching drill: "Tarnmittel und Zweige weg von der Mündung" ("camouflage and branches away from the muzzle"). By contrast, HEA12 p.8 says the 23/28's graze spring is stiffer than the standard A.Z. 23's: it needs 99–106 oz (2.8–3.0 kgf, DERIVED) to fire against 16–19 oz. Its graze action is therefore less sensitive than the base model's, while the direct-action nose still fires on contact.
- **Light targets versus armour (Fibel p.71, "Die guten Sieben").**
  - **o.V.:** bursts on contact, with fragment spread "20 m nach beiden Seiten und 10 m vorwärts" (20 m to each side, 10 m forward). Listed against guns and their shields, wheels, tracks and vision slits. It sets fires and overturns vehicles.
  - **m.V.:** "Mine bei senkrechtem Auftreffen. Dringt erst ein und sprengt Holzbunker, Häuser, Unterstände, Wald und junge Panzer." On a square-on hit it acts as a mine: it penetrates first, then bursts. Listed against timber bunkers, houses, dugouts, woodland and light tanks.
  - **m.V. on a flat impact against firm ground:** ricochets, flies about 50 m further, then bursts 4–8 m above the target position.
  - No source gives a separate figure for brick or soil.

### Gr. 39 HL (shaped charge)

- **Fuze:** nose, A.Z. 38 (TM53 p.444) or A.Z. 38 St. (Brit43), point detonating. No delay setting is recorded.
- **Light obstacles.** Fibel p.71: "Nicht verwenden, wenn Tarnung, Zweige, Schutznetze vor dem Ziele sind. Sonst geht sie zu früh los!" Do not use it when camouflage, branches or nets are in front of the target, or it functions too early. Use it against the heaviest armour out to 1,000 m. Its dispersion is large and it flies slowly, so the sight is set ¼ farther, for example 750 m instead of 600 m.
- NV5 (secondary) says it could be carried in place of the Sprgr. and used against armour or as HE.

### Cartridge and primer

- Brit43: the gun takes "normal Flak 18 and 36 ammunition … fitted with C/22 electric primers instead of C/12 percussion primers". The cartridge case is design No. 6347.
- Fibel p.54: "Munition mit Schlagzündschraube gibt Kurzschluß!" ("Ammunition with a percussion primer causes a short circuit!")
- Fibel p.56: with Flak ammunition, replace the percussion primer screw (Schlagzündschraube) with the electric one (Glühzündschraube).

## Issue and availability

- **Aug 1943.** Fibel p.54 lists exactly four issued types: Pzgr. 39, Pzgr. 40, HL-Granate and Sprenggranate.
- **North Africa, 1943.**
  - The captured document M.I.10 A/M564 (via Brit43) gives the mix in use as HE 25 %, APCBC 66 %, AP 40 9 %.
  - The Tiger the British examined held only APCBC and percussion-fuzed HE (Brit43).
  - TTT34 likewise reports "nose-fuze HE and APCBC-HE".
- **Pzgr. 40.** Carried "occasionally, when available, a few rounds" (NV5 [S]); 9 % in the North Africa mix. No primary source I read gives the date it was introduced to the KwK 36.
- **Gr. 39 HL.** Appears in Brit43's 1943 table (as "H.E./A.Tk") and in Fibel (Aug 1943). TM53 lists it for the Kw.K. 36 only.
- **Time-fuzed HE.** Listed for the Kw.K. 36 (TM53) but "not likely to be encountered" (Brit43).

## Load-out and stowage

- **Total: 92 rounds**, all horizontal in unarmoured sheet-metal bins. Brit43 itemises them:

  | Location | Rounds |
  |---|---|
  | Left of driver | 6 |
  | Forward compartment, each side | 16 + 16 |
  | Forward fighting compartment, each side | 4 + 4 |
  | Aft fighting compartment, each side | 16 + 16 |
  | Fighting compartment floor | 4 + 4 |
  | Under the turntable | 6 |
  | **Total** | **92** |

  NV5 [S] groups the same 92 as 64 in the panniers, 16 along the hull sides, 6 under the turret floor and 6 beside the driver. TTT34 also gives 92.
- **Command Tiger: 66 rounds** (NV5 [S]).
- **From Oct 1944**, units were authorised to stow 16 more rounds along the hull sides (NV5 [S]). I found no primary source for this.
- **Mix.** The 1943 North Africa document gives about 23 HE, 61 APCBC and 8 AP 40 per 92 (Brit43's own arithmetic). NV5 [S] gives a "recommended ratio" of 50 % Pzgr. 39 and 50 % Sprgr., with a few Pzgr. 40 when available and Gr. 39 HL in place of Sprgr. I found no German primary source for the 50/50 ratio.

1. **Burst core mass (`88mm_APHE_ring`).** READ: `core_mass` 0.060 kg, labelled a 0.059–0.064 kg range. CITED: TM53 p.448, the entry that names the Kw.K. 36, gives ¼ lb ≈ **113 g** cyclonite/wax. The 60 g figure is CITED only for the Flak 41 round (TTT30; TM53 p.438). The repo's value matches the Flak 41 figure, not the KwK 36 entry.
2. **`round_type` and the 773 m/s note cite en.wikipedia.org** (READ, in `88mm_AP` and `88mm_APHE_ring`). CITED: TM53 pp.447–448 (APCBC with filler) and NV5 p.17 (773 m/s) own those facts.
3. **Pzgr. 39 mass.** READ: 10.2 kg from NV5 p.17, which agrees with CITED NV5 and TTT30. TM53 p.448 says 22 lb (9.98 kg), and the 1943 Flak-pattern AP is 20.71–20.75 lb (9.4 kg). The sources disagree; the repo's value is not wrong.
4. **`base_mass` 0.33 kg** (READ). CITED: HEA12 gives the Bd.Z. 5127 with tracer as 11½ oz = 326 g, which is consistent. But that fuze is the 5127; the KwK 36 Pzgr. 39 carries the 5103 or 5103/1 (TM53 p.448), whose mass I did not find. E9-369A gives the older Flak Bd.Z. as 2.18 lb (0.99 kg).
5. **`cavity_diameter` 0.0357 m** (READ, Tuned, "core diameter of the round's predecessor"). It equals the **Pzgr. 40 tungsten core's 1.406 in = 35.7 mm** (TM53 p.440), which is also `88mm_APCR`'s penetrator calibre. This may be a mix-up; I could not check the cited doc, which was excluded from this task.
6. **`arming_spend` 35 mm and `delay` 1–2 ms** (READ, from L.Dv. 487). I could not locate L.Dv. 487, and no other source I read gives a plate thickness or delay time for the 5103 or 5127. Unverified.
7. **`88mm_APCR` penetrator calibre 0.0357 m** (READ) matches TM53 (1.406 in). Brit43 gives 1.375 in (34.9 mm). The sources disagree. The 7.3 kg mass and 1.93 kg core match CITED TM53, Brit43 and NV5.
8. **Missing rounds.** There is no file for the **Sprgr. L/4.5 HE** (nose A.Z. 23/28, o.V./m.V.) or for the **Gr. 39 HL** (nose A.Z. 38). Both were issued (Fibel pp.54, 71; TM53 pp.444–445).
9. **`88mm_AP` is a solid fixture.** The issued Pzgr. 39 carries a filler. The file labels this as a fixture, so this is a note rather than a mismatch.
10. **The APCR comment's "record's about 1.4"** is consistent with NV5's table: 171/120 = 1.43 at 100 m, falling to 110/83 = 1.33 at 2,000 m (DERIVED).
