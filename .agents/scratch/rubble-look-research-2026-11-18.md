# Rubble look and interaction: techniques from shipped games

This is about the game's destruction model only: buildings, debris and ground.

**Source tags:** **P** = primary source I read directly. **P\*** = primary source, but I saw only a search excerpt because the site blocked me. **S** = secondary (press, wiki, forum). **O** = someone's observation of the game.

**Sites that blocked me:** fema.gov and highways.dot.gov (403), IndieDB and ModDB (Cloudflare), the Steam announcement (connection reset), gamedev.net (403), studylib (429). The PC Gamer CoH3 article came back without its body. I watched no videos.

## 1. Techniques

| 1 | Mud height texture over the base heightfield, only where deformed | Spintires/MudRunner, Zagrebelnyy. P: [Mud and Water of Spintires](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/mud-and-water-of-spintires-mudrunner), P: [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/mudrunner-porting-a-successful-title-to-console) | Each 16 m block has a 25×25 base texture and, only if muddy, a 128×128 mud texture: R = mud height over base, GB = "mud offset" (mud sliding away from wheels), A = blend from terrain shader to mud shader. The CPU draws wheel and chassis "primitives" into it; the author calls this "pure empirics". A denser vertex grid is used for the mud's look only. | Simulated only in active blocks near the truck; mud texture allocated only where needed; displacement on the GPU | Interaction, sludge look |
| 2 | Rut and berm stamps | Spintires, older article. P\*: [Rendering and simulation in offroad driving game](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/rendering-and-simulation-in-offroad-driving-game) | "Extrude" stamps blended with MIN cut ruts; "push" stamps blended with MAX raise the mound ahead of a wheel. Mud exists only where a truck has deformed it. | Trivial | Sludge look: sharp ruts and rims read as soil |
| 3 | Rigid chunks on the soft surface that break smaller when driven over | Spintires. P: same article as row 1 | Mud chunks are each "a full-fledged rigid body" and break into smaller chunks under wheels. | 4 | Ground types that can or cannot be deformed | SnowRunner. A fill pass refills tracks over time. | Xbox One: 0.011 + 0.175, labelled "ns" (probably ms) | Sludge look |
| 6 | Capture from below, plus global detail displacement | Batman: Arkham Origins. The PC version tessellates and adds an extra detail displacement from the normal map everywhere; consoles fake depth with relief mapping. | Two surfaces updated per frame | Sludge look |
| 7 | Heightfield granular model: displace to the nearest free ring, then erode with roughness | Sumner, O'Brien & Hodgins 1999. P: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.08683v1) | Columns under an object are lowered; displaced volume (less a compressed share α) goes to the nearest ring of free columns. The authors' stated limit: the model "can only generate smooth surfaces". | Linear in the object's projected area; only active columns are stored, in a hash | Interaction (this is our bed); sludge look (σ, θ_in) |
| 8 | Height Span Map: fill lying on, against and over objects; texture sliding | Onoue & Nishita 2003. | 9 | SCM soil with bulldozing | Project Chrono. P: [API docs](https://api.projectchrono.org/classchrono_1_1vehicle_1_1_s_c_m_deformable_terrain.html), P: [Batagoda et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04371) | Nodes are ray-cast and sink by Bekker/Mohr/Janosi soil laws. Bulldozing pushes the pressed volume to the rut sides with an erosion angle (55° in the demo), a flow factor and a set number of rings. | "RTF of 1.0 and below" for basic runs, but "movable rocks … RTF … as high as 30 to 40" (30–40× slower than real time) | Interaction; cost warning about rigid rocks on soft soil |
| 10 | Poured material joins the terrain when it lands | RoadCraft. **Facts behind flatness:**
- **Debris volume:** about 0.33 × the building's outer volume, "air space" being the reason the factor is that low. P\*: [FEMA 329](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/fema_329_debris-estimating_field-guide_9-1-2010.pdf).
- **Bulking:** broken masonry swells 67%, from 2,325 kg/m³ in place to 1,395 kg/m³ loose, which is a packing of 0.60. P\*: [FHWA earthwork](https://highways.dot.gov/federal-lands/pddm/dpg/earthwork-design).
- **Spread:** debris reaches H/8 to H/2 beyond the footprint (H = building height; Kobe earthquake data). S: [Springer 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13753-024-00600-7).
- **Shape:** collapsed buildings typically form lean-to, V or pancake shapes, with floors leaning on whatever survives. P\*: [FEMA US&R module 1c](https://www.fema.gov/pdf/emergency/usr/module1c2.pdf).

**What the bed shows against those facts (DERIVED unless marked):**
- **Volume is roughly honest.** house_1 is 4 × 6 m with eaves at 2.475 m and a ridge 1.5 m higher (`tools/constructions/house_1.house.ron`), so about 77 m³ of outer volume. FEMA's factor predicts about 25 m³ of debris. The bed holds 8.82 m³ of material (from `rubble-bed-fair-test.md`) ÷ 0.60 = 14.7 m³, which is 58% of FEMA's figure, and house_1 has no floors or contents, which FEMA's figure counts.
- **Spread is roughly honest.** The bed covered 57–71 m² after a pass; the Kobe range gives about 31–80 m² for this house.
- **So a hollow one-storey house turned entirely into fill really is a thin layer:** about 0.24 m on average and 0.78 m at most (MEASURED). If nothing spread it, the same volume would stand as a 2 m cone at 38°.
- **The height of real ruins comes from solid pieces, not from fill.** In our code, `server_body` (`src/debris/crumble.rs:234-237`) sizes a fragment by the cube root of its volume. A whole 6.6 m roof board, 0.2 × 0.05 m in section, scores 0.40 m, under the 0.5 m floor, so it becomes fill at brick density.

## 2. The five most promising for us, ranked

1. **Keep long and large pieces, and wall stubs, as solids; heap the fill around them.**
   - Every shipped game whose ruins read as ruins keeps solids standing: BF6's core, BC2's girders and wall segments, CoH's frame, The Finals' skeleton (rows 12–15).
   - The missing law: a piece counts as granular only if it is small in all three dimensions compared with the patch that loads it. A board or a slab bridges and stands proud.
   - How: a piece that has come to rest becomes static in one direction only. Your standing ruling says settled rubble may rejoin the structure, and a one-way change avoids the flip-flop the settle experiment hit.
   - Fill banks against these solids and over them, using Onoue's height spans (row 8) or the plan's upward ray. A solid leaves only by breaking under load, in stages (row 16). A solid resting on fill sinks into it through the bearing law.
   - This addresses flatness, look and interaction. On the server it costs static colliders and no awake bodies.
   - **Risk:** a static slab on bare ground could beach the tank again (#374) unless it can break. That is the deferred press pass.

2. **Scatter chunks from bed depth, the way Horizon places rocks, with each chunk fixed at its own burial depth.**
   - The plan's chunks "follow the bed top each frame" and "sink and rise with it". Things riding a moving surface are what floating debris on liquid looks like, and I think that is the main reason it reads as sludge.
   - Rubble chunks *are* the surface. They get pressed down or crushed; they do not bob.
   - Each chunk's spot on a world grid gets a threshold from a fixed pattern (row 20), so chunks appear and vanish in a fixed order as depth changes. Each one keeps the depth it was buried at, shows when the fill above it goes, and when a track presses it, it moves to the pressed surface and stays there.
   - It costs nothing on the server or the wire. Late joiners see the same thing, which today's sheds cannot give them, and the 1,024 cap stops being tied to sheds.

3. **Draw the surface finer than its collision, with a rubble material.**
   - MudRunner uses a separate dense mesh just for the look (row 1); Batman adds detail displacement everywhere (row 6).
   - The bed could get a fixed-pattern noise displacement scaled by depth, on the client only, with uneven normals and textures blended by height between brick, mortar and dust.
   - Its edge should fade into the ground (Spintires' alpha blend, row 22), with a brick-dust tint on the ground beyond the toe (row 21).
   - This attacks the smooth, single-colour look that reads as a pool, and costs only client GPU time.

4. **Make it hold a print.**
   - A liquid cannot hold a print. Steep rut walls, raised rims and tread marks are what make ground read as granular: Rise of the Tomb Raider's three states (row 5), Spintires' MIN/MAX stamps (row 2), Chrono's 55° erosion angle (row 9), Sumner's θ_in steeper than θ_out (row 7).
   - On the server, the bearing law's inner wall would follow the material's own inner slope. That angle for coarse brick rubble is Unknown until a geotechnical source settles it.
   - On the client, track prints on the bed should come forward from "wait"; they already exist in `src/track_prints.rs`.

5. **Crush in stages instead of turning the whole pile into fill in one pass.**
   - The fair test had 7 bodies after collapse and 0 after the first pass (MEASURED), so one pass flattened everything.
   - Instruments of Destruction (row 16) and Spintires (row 3) break pieces into the next size down. Only pieces small in every dimension should join the fill.
   - To keep server cost bounded, small pieces skip collisions with other debris (row 16) and large ones go static under #1.

## 3. What nobody seems to do, and why that might be

- **A server-authoritative bed of granular building debris that heavy vehicles bear on: I found no one doing this.**
  - Destruction leftovers in shipped games are rigid bodies with caps, timers or removal (The Finals, Teardown, Red Faction, IoD, Chaos), or authored ruin swaps (Arma, CoH, BC2).
  - Deformable granular ground in shipped games is terrain that artists paint, mostly worked out by feel (Spintires calls it "pure empirics") and simulated on one machine. The closest case is RoadCraft's sand joining the terrain when it lands.
  - Likely reasons:
    - Rigid pieces on soft soil are the expensive case (Chrono runs 30–40× slower than real time with movable rocks).
    - Designers want debris layouts players can learn (BF6).
    - Bandwidth: The Finals averaged about 400 kbit/s in a large collapse before compression; Teardown calls voxel data "simply too much data".
  - For scale: the bed design guessed 104–208 kbit/s per client while a hull sinks (DERIVED, never measured). That is in the same range as The Finals' compressed peak of about 175 kbit/s.
- **Conserving volume from building to pile: no one.** Pile heights are authored, or are whatever the rigid pieces stack to. Cover height and whether a ruin is passable are design choices (CoH's end states, Embark tuning rubble for traversal). FEMA gives the honest figure.
- **Making collision match the visible rubble: no one.** Visible detail (chunks, particles, relief mapping) sits on a smoothed collision surface (Spintires' decorative particles, Batman's console fallback). Small rigid debris under heavy vehicles looks like toys (Iron Command) and costs a lot (Chrono). That supports the plan's split: a smooth surface for collision, rubble for the eye.
- **Turning settled debris into static, still-breakable structure: no talk describes it.** Chaos and IoD remove settled debris instead. The Finals' debris piles are documented in one line only. My guess at the reason: a one-way change needs a way to break the piece that never flips it back to dynamic, which is exactly the trap the settle experiment fell into.
